# Conversion optimization and A/B testing

## Context and recommendation

This site is a Next.js static export (`output: 'export'`) deployed as static files, and its root layout already loads Google Analytics 4 (GA4). Static hosting keeps the site simple and inexpensive, but it does not provide a Next.js server at request time to assign a visitor to a variant. A test would need client-side assignment or infrastructure at a CDN/edge layer. Client-side tests also need care to avoid briefly showing the original page before changing it (a “flicker”).

**Recommendation:** do not add an experimentation platform until GA4 shows enough eligible visits and completed conversions to answer a useful question in a reasonable time. First inspect the donation and volunteer journeys, fix clear usability or accessibility issues, and speak with users. If a test is supportable and GA4 must remain the source of truth, trial GrowthBook and validate its GA4/BigQuery setup. If a single analytics-and-experiment product is preferable, consider PostHog and its current free limits. Neither product removes the need to implement and review the variants, consent behavior, privacy protections, or accessible interactions.

The repository shows that GA4 is installed, but does not contain traffic or conversion reports. The site’s testing capacity therefore cannot be determined from the codebase; use actual eligible traffic and conversion counts from GA4 before choosing a tool.

## GA4 integration

GA4 can receive an experiment exposure event and variant identifier from an external tool, but that event collection alone does not provide variant assignment or a complete experiment analysis workflow. GrowthBook documents a path using GA4 exposure events and a BigQuery export. PostHog’s built-in analysis expects events captured in PostHog, so using it alongside GA4 may mean maintaining two analytics event streams. BigQuery and other associated services should be checked for their current quotas and charges.

Before any integration, agree on one canonical conversion event (for example, a completed donation or completed volunteer inquiry), deduplicate repeat events where appropriate, and verify that assignment and conversion events are recorded consistently. Do not send personal information in analytics event names or parameters. The existing GA4 script does not by itself establish that consent, retention, or privacy settings are appropriate; verify these in the relevant account and with the organization’s privacy requirements. [Google Analytics integration guidance](https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/collection/ga4/integration)

## Can site traffic support a test?

The repository does not include GA4 reports, so no conclusion about actual traffic is possible. Check monthly eligible visitors and baseline completed-conversion rate for the exact page and audience under consideration—not just overall page views. Then define the smallest improvement worth acting on, the test duration, and a stopping rule before looking at results.

As an illustration, detecting a conversion-rate change from **5% to 6%** (a 20% relative lift) with a conventional two-sided 5% significance level and 80% power takes approximately **8,000 eligible visitors per variant**. At 1,000 eligible visitors per month split evenly, that is about **16 months** to reach the illustrative sample, before accounting for exclusions, repeat visitors, seasonality, or other complications. The calculation is only an example: a different baseline or minimum worthwhile effect can change the sample requirement substantially. Use a sample-size calculator with the site’s real baseline and an agreed minimum detectable effect before committing to a test. [Evan Miller sample-size calculator](https://www.evanmiller.org/ab-testing/sample-size.html)

If the needed sample cannot be reached in a useful time, avoid declaring a noisy split-test winner. Prefer a clear, evidence-informed improvement and monitor it over time while accounting for seasonality; that is useful operational monitoring, not proof of causation.

## Practical CRO without A/B tests

- Review the donation and volunteer journeys on mobile and desktop. Remove avoidable steps, make the next action clear, and check that forms and payment handoffs work.
- Test key tasks with a small number of representative users and ask where they hesitate or misunderstand. Small qualitative studies find usability problems; they do not measure conversion lift. [NN/g guidance on usability-study participants](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-many-test-users/)
- Use GA4 funnel and page-level reports to locate potential drop-offs, while recognizing that analytics alone cannot explain why a person left.
- If approved by the organization’s privacy process, use a behavior analytics tool such as Clarity for qualitative signals. Mask sensitive fields and do not treat recordings or heatmaps as causal evidence.
- Make changes that are supported by user feedback, accessibility review, and obvious journey friction; track the chosen outcome and contextual factors after launch.

## Accessibility and privacy safeguards

An experimentation or analytics platform does not make variants accessible or privacy-compliant automatically. Review every variant with keyboard-only and screen-reader testing and against the organization’s applicable WCAG target. Keep essential content and functionality available if JavaScript or the experiment service fails; do not unexpectedly move focus, delay critical content, or rely on color alone to communicate a variant-specific state. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)

Minimize collected data, never include personal information in event payloads, and explicitly review consent, masking, retention, access, and vendor data handling—especially for donations and contact forms. Check whether a client-side experiment changes what third parties receive and whether that collection is permitted under the organization’s policies. Vendor privacy features are controls to configure, not a determination that a deployment complies with applicable law.

## Suggested decision

1. Use GA4 to establish eligible monthly visits and completed conversions for one high-value journey; confirm event quality and privacy settings.
2. Address clear accessibility and usability problems and gather qualitative feedback before splitting already-limited traffic.
3. If a sample-size calculation supports a decision within a useful timeframe, pilot GrowthBook for the GA4/BigQuery workflow, or PostHog if consolidating events and experiment reporting is preferred. Check current free-tier quotas and assign an owner for setup and maintenance.
4. If the required sample is out of reach, skip A/B testing for now. Make a measured, user-informed improvement and monitor the outcome without claiming the change caused it.
